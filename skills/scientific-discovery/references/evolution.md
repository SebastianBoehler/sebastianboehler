# Improve the skill from evidence

Use when skill maintenance is authorized. Consider explicit user feedback, reproducible workflow
failures, or observations that transfer beyond a single project's settings. Ordinary research and a
surprising score alone do not authorize edits or establish a general lesson.

- Record the observation, origin, expected/actual behavior, alternative explanation, proposed narrow
  change, and limits in the evidence notes. Use public-safe descriptions and artifact pointers.
- Distinguish user preference, hypothesis, supported procedure, and counterexample. A preference can
  guide behavior without becoming an empirical claim.
- Edit the smallest useful rule, pruning duplication or obsolete restrictions. Keep project-specific
  choices in their project. Preserve scope, evidence, permissions, and publication constraints.
- For a behavioral change, use a realistic case that checks decisions rather than required wording.
  Retain regression cases. When available and authorized, forward-test the pinned revision in a fresh
  context with raw facts, without supplying the intended answer or rubric. Use a control when practical.
- Validate frontmatter, links, portability, and scaffold text. Inspect artifacts and actual actions;
  record failures and skipped checks. A structural check or plan-only case does not establish better
  research outcomes or cross-model reliability.

Keep a reversible diff, identify the tested revision, and bump metadata for a material behavior change.
Commit or publish within existing authorization. Promote recurring, transferable observations or
explicit preferences with their boundaries; leave uncertain explanations as hypotheses. Updates need
actual edits and validation, not a promise of automatic learning.

A compact evidence entry can record: date/revision; observation and evidence status; limits; narrow
change; case and result; retain/revise/revert decision. Keep private data out of shared notes and
avoid tuning the skill only to a familiar handful of examples.
