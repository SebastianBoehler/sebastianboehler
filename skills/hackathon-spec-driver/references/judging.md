# Assess demo and judging fit

Use when choosing a competitive direction or reviewing a submission. Check official criteria,
weights, eligibility, and what judges or voters can inspect. Sponsor objectives and judges' public
work can suggest questions; label these as hypotheses, not private preferences or scoring rules.
Limit research to information that can change the build or presentation.

Evaluate the exact commit, deployment, submission fields, and video that form the deadline artifact.
Separate eligibility, rubric fit, audience comprehension, and evidence credibility. Compare with
relevant official winners and discoverable entrants, disclosing missing rosters, private submissions,
unknown weights, and organizer discretion. Public repositories are only part of the comparison set.

For a substantial review, report the strongest and weakest dimensions, a useful product improvement,
a useful demo improvement, and the evidence or uncertainty behind the assessment. Use a competitive
tier when justified; give a probability range only if requested and supported. An agent's own score
is not a calibrated placement forecast.

For peer voting, check whether an uninformed viewer can explain the product, recall its name and
novelty, describe the result, and see a reason to vote after a short exposure. Adapt the test to the
presentation format; technical sophistication alone does not establish audience impact.

When results arrive, compare the prediction and evaluated snapshot with the actual outcome. Record
specific evidence and plausible error causes without turning one contest into a universal rule.
