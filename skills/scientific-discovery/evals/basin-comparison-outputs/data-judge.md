# Blind review: data-first diagnosis plans

This is a plan-only review. Neither response reports an actual inspection, measurement, or experiment. Both correctly say that the chat-reported scores and runtimes are unverified and confounded by simultaneous changes to data, code, and preprocessing. Neither claims to have identified a bottleneck or established that the new configuration caused the reported differences.

## Response 1

- **Comparability and provenance:** Strong coverage. It asks for run and checkpoint identities, logs, commands, revisions and uncommitted changes, dependencies, seeds, hardware, elapsed-time definitions, metric, split, data snapshots, and preprocessing. It distinguishes identity hashes from correctness and calls for a matched one-factor comparison where feasible. Missing provenance leads to an isolated baseline or an explicitly unresolved diagnosis.
- **Measurement before intervention:** It profiles the active path on the available machine, including loading, preprocessing, transfers, training, evaluation, end-to-end time, utilization, and host/GPU memory. It calls for GPU synchronization and separates cold-start costs. A measured dominant stage is a condition for a targeted intervention. A short slice is explicitly limited by its representativeness; the plan does not establish in advance that the allocated time can capture full-run behavior.
- **Budget and delegation:** The table sums to 110 coordinator plus 70 assistant minutes, or 180 combined engineering-minutes. The assistant brief partitions its cap into 35, 25, and 10 minutes and specifies deliverables, isolation, and acceptance checks. The coordinator's 35-minute comparison may not fit a meaningful full training or held-out quality run; the plan recognizes that possibility and stops rather than claiming a result.
- **Correctness and constraints:** It checks inputs, workload, split, evaluator, output shapes and units, and numerical tolerance, with correctness unresolved if a task-appropriate tolerance is unavailable. It preserves original inputs and run outputs, avoids editing running jobs, serializes GPU-intensive tests, enforces memory limits, and prohibits external data sharing or paid resources. It notes the risk of repeated selection on held-out data.
- **Trade-offs and omissions:** The breadth of the data audit, provenance reconstruction, profiling, and matched test is ambitious for the time caps; useful prioritization will depend on what artifacts already exist. It calls for independent units and uncertainty assessment, but does not promise that enough independent observations or repeated runs will fit. Its comparison and correctness checks are conditional, so the plan may reasonably end without a verified bottleneck or quality conclusion.

## Response 2

- **Comparability and provenance:** Strong coverage. The coordinator verifies run artifacts, revisions, preprocessing, split, evaluator, checkpoint, score, elapsed-time definition, hardware, sample count, steps, precision, and overhead. The assistant checks actual configurations, fit boundaries, leakage, units, masks, and available inference information. The decision rules explicitly state that validated historical results still cannot isolate a cause when all three factors changed. If artifacts are missing, it asks for them or proposes a later known-configuration rerun.
- **Measurement before intervention:** The assistant is assigned a bounded benchmark and stage profile on representative local work, with warmup, GPU synchronization, end-to-end time, memory, and repeated paired measurements if feasible. It separates instrumented and uninstrumented totals and warns that low utilization alone does not establish cause. The next action depends on measured evidence, and a short timing slice is not treated as full-training quality evidence.
- **Budget and delegation:** The allocations total 180 combined engineering-minutes; the assistant's 25 + 25 + 35 minutes match its 85-minute brief. Each block has an owner and deliverable. The table already charges both owners for concurrent work, so its instruction to shorten later blocks if work overlaps is unnecessarily conservative or ambiguous; overlap itself does not require shortening if both owners' time is already counted. The comparison and full rerun are appropriately conditional on remaining time and artifacts.
- **Correctness and constraints:** It checks shapes, finite values, units, preprocessing, metric inputs, and trustworthy existing outputs where available. It keeps the split and metric fixed, requires same-work comparisons, records memory, and preserves inputs and historical outputs in an isolated directory without external sharing or paid resources. It distinguishes 64 GB system RAM from unspecified GPU memory. Unlike Response 1, it does not explicitly define an acceptable numerical tolerance or explicitly coordinate GPU-intensive work to avoid interference with other work or running jobs; these would strengthen the measurement and job safeguards.
- **Trade-offs and omissions:** The 85-minute assistant scope is substantial, and the setup plus profiling caps may not capture startup, epoch boundaries, evaluation, and representative training if those stages are slow. The response acknowledges that limit and permits an unresolved bottleneck. It calls for uncertainty reporting, but does not specify independent data units for assessing score uncertainty. It also leaves a controlled remedy test optional, which is appropriate if evidence or time is insufficient.

## Comparison

Both plans meet the central ordering requirement: establish provenance and measure the actual workload before choosing a remedy. Both provide a bounded assistant brief and a feasible accounting of 180 combined person-minutes, subject to the unknown cost of the actual workload. Response 1 is more explicit about numerical tolerances, independent units, and avoiding interference with running jobs. Response 2 gives a more granular owner-by-owner schedule and explicitly distinguishes system RAM from GPU memory and profiler overhead from end-to-end time. These are trade-offs rather than grounds for a forced overall winner. Neither plan demonstrates that a full matched training and quality comparison will fit; both correctly allow a limited or unresolved conclusion.
